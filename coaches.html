Women's team coach

Jamie Sinclair

A Ravens national champion who went on to skip at two world championships, Jamie brings world-class experience back to the program where she won her first national title.

Jamie grew up in Manotick, just south of Ottawa, and won gold at the 2007 Canada Winter Games playing lead for Rachel Homan. She won two Ontario junior championships as a skip before coming to Carleton, where she skipped the Ravens women to the 2014 CIS national championship, the program's first national curling title. She graduated from Carleton with an honours degree in International Business.

Jamie then moved to the United States to compete. She won three straight U.S. women's championships (2017–2019) and represented the United States at the 2018 and 2019 World Women's Championships. In 2018 her team became the first American team to win a Grand Slam of Curling event, the Players' Championship. She was runner-up at the U.S. Olympic Trials in 2017 (women's) and 2021 (mixed doubles). Since returning to Canada in 2022 she has played in the Scotties Tournament of Hearts in 2024 and 2025.

Off the ice, Jamie is a curling analyst for NBC Sports. She called the 2018, 2022 and 2026 Winter Olympics, and was part of NBC's Sports Emmy-winning broadcast team in 2022. She is an NCCP Level 2 certified coach and has coached teams at the club and provincial levels.

Jamie leads the women's team in 2026–27. In 2025–26, while she was in Italy calling the Milan Cortina Olympics for NBC, Kim Wilson coached the team.

Coaching and lessons

Curl Up With Jamie

Jamie's coaching company, Curl Up With Jamie, makes curling more approachable for players of every age and level, from first-season club curlers to competitive teams. She breaks complex skills into simple, practical steps, both on the ice and on her YouTube channel.

  • Private lessons

    50-minute one-on-one sessions on delivery, sweeping, strategy and shot-making, using video analysis, laser line-of-delivery checks and speed timing.

  • Group lessons

    50-minute sessions for up to four players, focused on communication, team systems, shot selection and position-specific skills.

  • Club clinics

    Custom clinics for curling clubs, from a two-hour strategy or sweeping session to a full weekend covering every part of the game.

  • Rise & Slide

    A six-week early-morning development program at the Manotick Curling Club, Wednesdays 6:30–8:00 am, with fall and winter sessions.

  • Virtual training

    50-minute one-on-one online coaching sessions by video call, tailored to your skill level.

  • YouTube tutorials

    Free curling tutorials and tips on the Curl Up With Jamie YouTube channel.

Men's team coach

Edward Cyr

A national university champion as a player, Edward coaches the Ravens men and teaches engineering at Carleton.

Edward grew up in Minden, in Ontario's Haliburton Highlands, and curled for the University of Waterloo Warriors while studying mechanical engineering. Playing third for skip Jake Walker, he won back-to-back OUA championships in 2012 and 2013, a silver medal at the 2012 CIS national championships, and the national title in 2013. He was named the first-team all-star third at both the OUA and CIS championships in 2012 and 2013.

Edward is an NCCP certified Competition coach. As head coach of the Ravens men, he led the team through the 2025–26 season. They won their first six games at the OUA championships and took silver, then reached the U SPORTS national final and won silver after a 7–6 loss to Calgary.

Edward joined Carleton's Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering in 2021, where he teaches courses including first-year engineering mechanics and dynamics. He holds a BASc and a PhD from the University of Waterloo, and before Carleton he was the inaugural McCain Postdoctoral Fellow in Innovation at the University of New Brunswick.

Edward has also called university curling for television. He was the CBC commentator for the 2019 U SPORTS/CCAA Curling Championships in Fredericton, N.B., and announced the men's final in which Cameron Goodkey's Ravens won Carleton's national title.
